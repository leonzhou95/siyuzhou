Digital Platforms, Identity & Mobility

My first line of research examines how mobile populations, including international students, expatriates, and new migrants, use digital platforms to perform identity and build social capital and social positioning across national and cultural boundaries.

This research interest is closely tied to my own experience. I spent a year as an undergraduate exchange student at Pontificia Universidad Javeriana in Bogotá, Colombia, from 2015 to 2016. As one of only two Chinese students on campus, and among very few in the country at the time, I learned firsthand what it takes to build a sense of belonging from scratch, often through digital connections back home and Facebook groups set up for international students. Later, while completing graduate studies in Spain, I spent a year working as a new media editor for Barcelona Television's Chinese-language channel. I grew its WeChat account from zero to more than 6,000 followers and watched it become one of the primary channels through which the local Chinese community stayed informed and connected from then on. That hands-on experience with how diasporic communities build identity and belonging through digital platforms continues to shape this line of research today.
